## 1. What SAP Sales Cloud V2 is: launch, technical platform, licensing and packaging

### Takeaway
SAP Sales Cloud Version 2 ("V2") is SAP's next-generation cloud CRM for sales force automation. It is a separate product, rebuilt from scratch, not a new release of SAP Cloud for Customer (C4C, now called "V1"). It runs as a multi-tenant SaaS on a cloud-native, microservices, API-first and event-driven stack. SAP's audit reports say it is hosted on AWS. V2 was already in productive use in the first half of 2023. I found no official "general availability" date. It is sold per named user, separately from SAP Service Cloud V2, which runs on the same platform. It is also bundled into some SAP Cloud ERP ("GROW with SAP" Premium) packages.

## 2. CRM (Sales Cloud V2) versus ERP (SAP S/4HANA), and how V2 quotes and orders relate to S/4HANA

### Takeaway
Sales Cloud V2 is the front-office system of engagement: accounts, contacts, leads, opportunities, pipeline, activities, visits, and quotes and orders as they are captured. S/4HANA stays the system of record for pricing conditions, availability (ATP), credit management, fulfilment and delivery, billing and invoicing. V2 integrates with S/4HANA in three ways:
- It calls S/4HANA synchronously to simulate pricing, ATP and credit for quotes and orders ("external pricing").
- It transfers orders to S/4HANA for execution.
- It receives replicated ERP orders, quotes, master data and document status for display.

Sales orders in V2 come in three documented modes: internal, bidirectional (simulate and transfer) and external (replicated, view-only).

### Cited Findings
- SAP Learning, "Exploring SAP Sales Cloud Version 2", lesson "Using Sales Orders", describes three sales order modes:
  - **Internal:** orders stay in Sales Cloud V2.
  - **Bidirectional:** created in V2, then simulated and transferred to S/4HANA. In simulations the rep receives "results for pricing, credit check, and so on".
  - **External:** replicated from SAP S/4HANA or SAP ERP, and only searched and viewed in V2.

### Inferences
- **Which processes live where.** This is a synthesis of the sources above; neither SAP nor the partners describe the split with these exact words.

  | Area | System | Basis |
  |---|---|---|
  | Account and contact engagement, leads, opportunities and pipeline, forecasting, activities and visits, playbooks | Sales Cloud V2 | Sources above |
  | Quote and order capture | Sales Cloud V2 | Sources above |
  | Pricing conditions, ATP, credit management | S/4HANA, called synchronously from V2 ("simulate") | Sources above |
  | Delivery and logistics, billing and invoicing, accounts receivable | S/4HANA | Status shown back in V2 according to partner sources |
  | Customer and product master data | Usually mastered in S/4HANA and replicated to V2 | Partner sources |
- For a non-expert: V2 is where a rep records and progresses a deal. When the rep needs a binding price, stock confirmation or a credit check, V2 asks S/4HANA. When the order is final it is handed to S/4HANA, which ships and invoices it.
- The deployment matters. Some flows (for example replicating ERP-originated quotes into V2) are supported for S/4HANA on-premise and Private Edition but not for Public Edition. An implementation has to check the scenario list in the integration guide for its specific S/4HANA edition.

## 3. Differences between V1 (C4C) and V2; upgrade or re-implementation; V1 maintenance commitment; migration tooling

### Takeaway
V2 is not an in-place upgrade. Moving from C4C/V1 is a transition, in practice a re-implementation. SAP provides tooling to move **data and some configuration**: a Readiness Check in V1, the Data Transfer Tool (DTT), and TransforMate for converting SDK code. Custom code (PDI/ABSL), mashups, workflow rules, integrations, UI extensions and reports have to be rebuilt in V2 or on SAP BTP. As of October 2026, **SAP has not announced an end-of-maintenance date for V1**. V1 still ships quarterly releases (2605, 2608, 2611), which increasingly focus on transition tooling. New innovation, especially AI, goes into V2.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For a company not yet on any SAP CRM, the V1/V2 distinction matters mainly because new implementations should be on V2. V1 (C4C) still works and is supported, but it receives mostly maintenance and transition tooling, while new features (AI, deal scoring, dynamic visit planning on mobile, offline retail execution) land in V2.
- For an existing V1 customer, the move is a project: new tenant, new license, data migration with the DTT, and a rebuild of extensions and integrations. It is not a technical upgrade.

### Gaps
- No official SAP statement (product page, roadmap or SAP Note) with a V1 end-of-maintenance date or a formal maintenance commitment was found. The "no date announced" position comes from community and partner sources.
- No official V1-to-V2 feature parity matrix was found. Parity gaps belong to the "limitations" researcher's scope.
- License conversion terms (credits, swaps) are not published. Only partner claims exist.

## 4. Core functionality: objects, processes, automation, mobile, groupware

### Takeaway
V2 covers the standard B2B sales force automation lead-to-order chain:
- accounts and contacts with timeline and account planning
- leads with playbooks
- opportunities with pipeline management and territories
- sales quotes and sales orders with internal or ERP pricing and ATP
- activities (appointments, phone calls, emails, tasks) and visits, including check-in and check-out, surveys and tasks, and dynamic visit and route planning

Automation runs on Autoflow rules for events, notifications, emails and approvals, with SAP Build Process Automation for complex approvals. Users work in a browser UI, a native iOS/Android app (largely online), a separate offline-capable SAP Retail Execution app, and server-side Outlook, Gmail and Microsoft Teams integrations. The 2026 releases added mostly AI-assisted features: email-to-order, deal tracker, AI deal scoring and account plan summaries.

## 5. A typical day of a sales representative in V2, according to SAP's own materials

### Takeaway
SAP's learning journey "Exploring SAP Sales Cloud Version 2" (free, about 4.5 hours, six units) has a dedicated unit on the sales representative's daily work: daily tasks, playbooks and guided selling, quotes, sales orders, task management with the digital selling workspace, and visit management. Separate units cover the sales manager, MS Teams and relationship intelligence, AI, and the mobile app. Combined with the 2026 mobile and AI features, these materials support a realistic day: plan, visit, record, quote, order, follow up. That day is reconstructed below, with each step tied to a source.

### Cited Findings
- Course structure: a sales representative unit covering daily tasks, playbooks and guided selling, quotes, sales orders, task management with the digital selling workspace, and visit management. ### Inferences
- Assembled from the sources above, a field B2B rep's day in V2 looks like this:
  1. Open Daily Catch-Up on the phone.
  2. Accept or re-optimize the dynamic visit route.
  3. Check in at each customer, run the assigned surveys and tasks, and take an order or create a follow-up from the visit.
  4. Log calls and meetings (or let AI summarize them).
  5. Progress opportunities along the playbook.
  6. Between visits, handle Outlook email with the add-in, including converting order emails into draft orders.
  7. Build quotes with S/4HANA pricing and ATP, send them for approval, and convert them to orders that are transferred to S/4HANA.
  8. Check order and invoice status replicated back from ERP.

  This sequence is my synthesis. SAP does not publish it as a single "day in the life" narrative in the sources found.
- A key-account or office-based rep's day would weigh account planning, the pipeline manager and Outlook/Teams more heavily, and visit execution less.

## 6. Consumer goods and food-industry features (retail execution, visits and surveys, perfect store, DSD)

### Takeaway
V2 has a consumer-goods field execution path through the **SAP Retail Execution** mobile app, a separate app built on SAP Mobile Services that requires V2 users. It offers merchandising and order entry, customer-specific pricing and discounts, store audits with task and survey objectives, and full **offline** visit execution. SAP reported it as GA in mid-2026; an older, undated help page calls it beta. V2's core visit features (surveys and tasks on visits, dynamic visit and route planning) apply to CG field sales too. The richer V1 "Perfect Store" toolkit (Consumer Product Extended scoping, engagement maps and points of engagement, the Imaging Intelligence planogram add-on) is documented for V1, and its parity in V2 is unconfirmed. **Direct Store Delivery (DSD)**, meaning van sales, delivery and route accounting, is an **S/4HANA** product, not a Sales Cloud V2 feature. I found no food-specific capabilities in V2 (perishables, catch weight, cold chain).

### Inferences
- For a food or consumer-goods company, the realistic SAP split as of October 2026 is:
  - **Sales Cloud V2:** key-account and field sales management, visits, surveys and tasks, route planning, opportunities and quotes, and order capture.
  - **SAP Retail Execution app on V2:** offline in-store merchandising, audits and order entry.
  - **S/4HANA:** pricing and ATP, fulfilment and invoicing. If van sales or route delivery are needed, that means **S/4HANA DSD**, which requires private cloud or on-premise S/4HANA.
- The V2 Retail Execution app is new (first released around 2024 based on its app ID; GA offline in mid-2026). Its maturity and functional depth against V1 Perfect Store should be validated in a demo.

- I found no V2-specific documentation of Perfect Store constructs (engagement maps, points of engagement, planogram image recognition) or of an "industry solution" scoping for consumer products in V2.
- I found no food-industry-specific V2 features (perishables or shelf-life, catch weight, cold-chain, route-to-market for traditional trade). Trade promotion management was not researched; SAP offers it as separate products.
- I found no V2 consumer-goods or food customer reference or case study. The search budget ran out before this could be checked.
- I could not verify the licensing of the Retail Execution app (included in V2 or an add-on), or whether it needs separate SAP Mobile Services or BTP subscriptions beyond "SAP Mobile Services enabled".
